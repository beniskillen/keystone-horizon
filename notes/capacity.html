The queue is the limit.

Management can scale. A steel-stud factory cannot pretend to. The offer is sized to the wall, which is the opposite of a launch calendar.

Research note, written for the file. Not an offer, not a forecast, and not a KHC return. Every figure below is someone else’s published statistic, with the source named. Australian wholesale rules are a gate, not a paragraph.

The number

Two factories, a priority queue, about four large new builds and one development of eight to ten units in a year. That is the 4 + 1 on the homepage. It came from the 18 September call. It is not a licence count, and it is not a promise that four buildings will be completed on a date. It is the reason a reader should distrust any version of KHC that starts to sound like a release every month.

What other operators optimise

Kiss optimises the launch. Sold out is the proof they put on the page, and a sold-out Saturday is a real commercial event. It measures demand for that release. It does not measure how many buildings a specification can pass through a factory. Magnum publishes a portfolio of eight developments and 575 residences since 2019, which is a different scale, in established streets, with a projected return on each project. Scale is their argument. A cap is ours. A buyer can prefer either argument. They should notice they are arguments about different constraints.

Why a cap is a research point

Oversupply in the short-stay book is already visible in the listing data: revenue per listing down while arrivals were up. Adding product without a reason is how a corridor becomes a brochure. A queue that stops at the steel is one way to stay off that treadmill. The other half is place. Building the next identical pool in the most photographed street is a different business from holding a small number of files in the window the strategy names.

If a later deck shows a pipeline that ignores the queue, believe the queue. Christopher’s methodology note, when he writes it, replaces this paragraph. Until then the cap stands as a discipline, not as a trophy.
