Look · The corridor

Faster growth is a place, not a percent you can buy.

Bali’s economy grew 5.82% in 2025. Australia’s grew 2.6% through the year to December 2025. Both are official. They are not the same object, and neither is a villa.

Research note, written for the file. Not an offer, not a forecast, and not a KHC return. Every figure below is someone else’s published statistic, with the source named. Australian wholesale rules are a gate, not a paragraph.

The two prints

BPS Bali announced calendar-year growth of 5.82% for 2025, the highest in seven years on their reading, above 5.60% in 2019 and above 5.48% in 2024. Nominal gross regional product was Rp177.99 trillion, against Rp168.20 trillion the year before. The fastest industry rates BPS named were government administration at 10.99%, and accommodation and food at 10.25%.

The ABS measured Australian GDP up 0.8% in the December quarter 2025 and 2.6% through the year. GDP per capita was up 0.9% through that same year. Bank Indonesia’s February 2026 provincial report put Bali’s 2026 range at 5.4–6.2%, against a national Indonesian range of 4.9–5.7%. That range is their projection. It is not a KHC forecast, and it is not a land price.

Read the bottom bar before the top one

Accommodation and food grew at 10.25%. Primary-market residential prices in Bali grew at 1.06% year on year in the fourth quarter of 2025. The economy that serves the guest is moving faster than the official price of a new house in the developer survey. Anyone who slides from “Bali is growing faster than Australia” to “therefore the villa compounds faster than a Sydney terrace” has changed the subject in the middle of the sentence.

GRDP is a province. GDP is a country. One is more tourism-weighted than the other. The comparison is still the right first chart, because it stops the corridor being described as a mood. The second chart, the house-price one, is in the price note.
