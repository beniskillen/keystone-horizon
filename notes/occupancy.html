Full, and more crowded.

Bali led Indonesia’s star hotels in 2025. The short-stay book is a different series, and it got paid less per listing while the airport got busier.

Research note, written for the file. Not an offer, not a forecast, and not a KHC return. Every figure below is someone else’s published statistic, with the source named. Australian wholesale rules are a gate, not a paragraph.

The hotel series

BPS room occupancy for star-rated hotels in 2025 was 61.02% in Bali and 49.30% for Indonesia. Bali was the fullest star-hotel province in that set. Use it when the asset is a star hotel. A villa with a photographer is not in the sample.

The listing series

Airbtics, as reported by Villa Finder Magazine, covered active Airbnb-style listings across Bali from November 2024 to October 2025. Median occupancy 65%. Average annual revenue about IDR 326 million a listing. Average daily rate about IDR 1.53 million. Monthly revenue down 6.13% on the year before. Foreign arrivals in the calendar year were up 9.72%. The guest showed up. The average listing did not get a raise. New supply is the ordinary explanation, and it is the one to test on any street that has a sold-out launch every quarter.

Villa Finder Magazine, citing Airbtics.

How this changes a pitch

If the pitch uses 61%, ask whether the building is a star hotel. If it uses a number in the seventies for a single street, ask for the sample, the year, and the costs underneath. If it ignores the revenue decline in the listing data, it is telling the arrival story and skipping the supply story. Both are published. A serious file quotes both, then shows why this asset is not the average listing.
